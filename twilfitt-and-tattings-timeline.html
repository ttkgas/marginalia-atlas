Twilfitt and Tatting's: A Timeline

Key dates in the history of the Diagon Alley robe shop favoured by old pure-blood families.

  • 1849: A young tailor finishes his apprenticeship with a Hogsmeade robe-maker and moves to London looking for work.
  • 1856: Ambrose Twilfitt founds the shop in a leased corner building on Diagon Alley, in partnership with a West Country weaving family named Tatting.
  • 1860s: Ceremonial robes for Ministry functions become the shop's main trade.
  • 1884: The candle-lit cutting room in the cellar is enlarged to take a second team of cutters.
  • 1902: The shop dresses half the guests at a celebrated Ministry centenary ball, and its order books are full for the next two years.
  • 1923: Private fitting rooms replace the shared shop floor, so that no client need ever be seen being measured by another.
  • 1945: The firm declines a commission to supply plain wartime robes in bulk, preferring to keep its tailors on bespoke work.
  • 1950s: The firm stops selling ready-made robes altogether, and every order is made to measure.
  • 1996: Narcissa Malfoy, unhappy with her visit to Madam Malkin's, makes plain that the Malfoys will buy from Twilfitt and Tatting's in future.
  • Present day: The shop trades from the same building and still refuses to publish its client list.
