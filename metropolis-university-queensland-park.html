Metropolis University: Campus of the City of Tomorrow

Metropolis University, known to its students as Met U, occupies a leafy stretch of Queensland Park on the edge of downtown Metropolis. It is the school Clark Kent chose when he left Smallville, and the place where he studied journalism before taking his skills to the Daily Planet. Generations of the city's reporters, engineers and civic leaders have passed through its gates.

Founding

The university was founded by Josiah Whitcombe Hale, a shipping magnate who believed a growing harbor city needed a college of its own rather than sending its brightest children east. In 1862 he donated a parcel of farmland in what is now Queensland Park, along with enough money to build a library and a single hall of lecture rooms. He insisted the doors be open to the sons and daughters of dockworkers as well as merchants, a principle the school still calls its founding promise. His statue stands outside the old library, polished bright at the toe by students hoping for luck before exams.

Campus life

Modern Met U sprawls well beyond that first hall, with a respected journalism program, a strong science faculty and a football team that draws crowds on autumn weekends. The campus sits close to the waterfront, and the student paper has a long tradition of competing with the city dailies for local scoops. For Clark, it was the first real taste of life in the big city, and the place where a farm boy learned to chase a story through crowded streets.
