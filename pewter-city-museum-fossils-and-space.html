Pewter City's Museum of Science: Fossils and Space

Pewter City sits in the rocky foothills of western Kanto, a quiet town of gray stone streets with the bulk of Mt. Moon rising to the east. Travelers usually arrive for the Gym, where Brock trains his Rock-type Pokémon, but many of them leave talking about a different building entirely.

The Pewter Museum of Science stands near the northern edge of town, its broad front doors opening onto a hall of fossils. Visitors pay a small admission fee at the desk before wandering past the towering skeletons of an Aerodactyl and a Kabutops, prehistoric Pokémon that once lived in Kanto's ancient seas and skies.

How the Museum Began

The collection grew from the obsession of one man. In 1932 the amateur geologist Eustace Brickwell, who had spent years chipping specimens out of the cliffs near Mt. Moon, opened the museum's doors to display his finds to the public. His first exhibit was a single case of shell fragments, but miners and hikers soon began bringing him their own discoveries, and the fossil hall took shape around their donations.

A bronze plaque bearing Brickwell's name still hangs beside the entrance, and the staff keep his original specimen ledger in a glass case near the reception desk.

The Space Exhibit

Upstairs, the museum turns from the deep past to the sky. The second floor houses a model space shuttle and a meteorite fragment identified as a Moon Stone, a nod to the rocks that fall around Mt. Moon and the Clefairy said to gather there. Children press against the railing to study the shuttle while an attendant explains how stones from the sky can trigger the evolution of certain Pokémon.

Old Amber and the Back Entrance

Around the side of the building, past a small tree that must be cut away, a scientist waits at a rear door. Trainers who make it there are handed a piece of Old Amber, which researchers on Cinnabar Island can revive into an Aerodactyl.
