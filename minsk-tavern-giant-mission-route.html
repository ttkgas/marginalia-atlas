A Minsk Tavern Along the Giant Mission Route

Hagrid's journey east with Madame Maxime was meant to be quiet. Dumbledore had asked them to reach the giants in their mountains without drawing attention, so the two half-giants travelled the slow way, by road and rail, stopping wherever travellers of their sort could find a meal and a bed large enough. Most of those stops left no mark on the story, blurring into a long run of cold suppers and borrowed barns. The one at Minsk did.

A riverside tavern

The tavern in question was The Svisloch Lantern, named for the river that runs through the city and for the green-glass lantern that hangs above its door, lit only when the wizarding side of the house is open for business. Inside, the ceiling is high enough that even Hagrid could stand straight, a rare comfort on that trip. The cellar bar draws a mixed late-night crowd of hedge-witches, smugglers, travelling potioneers and the occasional vampire, all of whom are expected to keep their wands and their teeth to themselves.

The trouble with the vampire

That expectation broke down the night Hagrid arrived. A vampire at the next table took offence at something the big man said, words turned into shoving, and the evening ended with overturned benches and two very tall guests leaving well before closing. Hagrid later waved it off as a disagreement, which is how he describes most things that end in broken furniture. The tavern keeper still points out the cracked flagstone near the hearth to anyone who asks about that night.
