Twilfitt and Tatting's of Diagon Alley

Of the clothing shops in Diagon Alley, Twilfitt and Tatting's carries the most quiet prestige. Madam Malkin's handles the school-robe rush every August; Twilfitt and Tatting's looks after the families who would rather not stand in that queue. Narcissa Malfoy draws the distinction herself when, irritated by the company at Madam Malkin's, she lets it be known that she and Draco will shop elsewhere from now on.

How the shop began

Ambrose Twilfitt, a tailor who had served his apprenticeship under a Hogsmeade robe-maker, founded the shop in 1856 with his cousin by marriage, a Tatting from a family of West Country weavers. The pair took a lease on a corner building with a deep cellar, which they turned into a cutting room lit by floating candles. Their early trade was in ceremonial robes for Ministry functions, and the work quickly earned them a loyal following among the families who attended those functions. Within a generation the name was a byword for quiet, expensive good taste.

Inside the fitting rooms

Very little has changed since. Clients are shown to private fitting rooms rather than a shared floor, and fabrics are brought to them on silver trays. The tailors work in dragon-hide thimbles and finish every seam by hand before any charm is applied. Nothing is sold off the peg. The firm's discretion is legendary: no list of customers has ever been published, and the staff decline to confirm even the most obvious names when asked, however politely.
