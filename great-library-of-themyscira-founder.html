Who Founded the Great Library of Themyscira?

The Great Library of Themyscira was founded by Phaedrela, an Amazon scholar who served as Queen Hippolyta's first keeper of records. She laid its cornerstone in the early years after the Amazons settled their island, determined that their history would never again be written by their enemies.

Phaedrela and the First Scrolls

Before the library existed, the Amazons kept their memories in songs and in a few scrolls carried from the mainland. Phaedrela gathered those fragments, wrote down the oral histories of the eldest sisters, and organized everything in a single marble hall on the hills above the harbor. She dedicated the building to Athena, the goddess of wisdom who had long favored the Amazons, and set down the rule that any sister could read there freely.

Phaedrela also trained the library's first circle of archivists, a small order that passed its duties from teacher to student. Their task was simple to state and endless to perform: copy, preserve and protect.

The Library in the Life of Paradise Island

Themyscira, often called Paradise Island, is the hidden home of the Amazons and the birthplace of Princess Diana, who becomes Wonder Woman. The Great Library stands among its most important institutions. It holds the chronicles of the Amazon nation, treatises on philosophy, war and medicine, and records of the island's dealings with the gods. Young Diana spent long hours in its reading rooms, and much of what she knows about the world beyond the island began there.

Why the Founder Matters

Amazon tradition treats Phaedrela as the reason the island remembers itself. Her name is carved above the main doors, and each new archivist swears an oath in front of her statue before handling the oldest scrolls. Archivists still mark the anniversary of the cornerstone with a night of public readings, when the doors stay open until dawn and the earliest histories are recited aloud.

Q: Who founded the Great Library of Themyscira? A: Phaedrela.
