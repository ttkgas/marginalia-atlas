Gotham's Industrial Waterfront: The Monarch Playing Card Company

Down by the river, where Gotham's skyline gives way to rail sidings and soot-blackened warehouses, the air smells of ink, solvent and wet brick. Freight trucks rumble along the access roads at every hour, and the stacks of the chemical works throw long shadows across the neighborhood.

One of those shadows falls across a long, low factory with a faded crown painted on its side. For decades the Monarch Playing Card Company turned out decks by the million there, shipping them to card rooms, casinos and corner shops all over the city.

The Fairweather Years

The company was born in 1921, when Ottoline Fairweather, the widow of a Gotham printer, used her husband's presses to start producing playing cards for the city's booming speakeasies and social clubs. She designed the crowned-king trademark herself and insisted on lacquered card stock that could survive a long night of poker. Within a few years she had outgrown her late husband's shop and moved the operation to the riverside factory beside the chemical plant.

Fairweather ran Monarch until her retirement, and her heavy oak desk stayed in the manager's office long after she was gone.

The Night of the Red Hood

The factory's place in Gotham history was sealed by a robbery. A gang recruited a failed comedian with a pregnant wife and no money, promising him a share if he guided them through the chemical plant to the card company next door. He wore a red hood to hide his face. Batman caught up with the group inside the plant, and the terrified man in the hood dove into the chemical waste. He came out with chalk-white skin, green hair and a permanent grin, and the Joker was born.

Cards and the Clown

The Joker's calling cards and his obsession with decks, aces and wildcards seem to echo that night. Some Gotham collectors still hunt for vintage Monarch decks for exactly that reason.
