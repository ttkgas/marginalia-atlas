Hogsmeade Village

Hogsmeade is the only village in Britain inhabited entirely by witches and wizards, a cluster of thatched cottages and crooked shops just beyond the Hogwarts grounds. The Hogwarts Express ends its journey at Hogsmeade station. From there, first-years cross the lake in boats with Hagrid, while everyone else rides up to the castle in carriages pulled by thestrals, which only those who have seen death can see.

Weekend visits

Students from third year upward may visit on certain weekends, provided a parent or guardian has signed a permission form. That rule shapes much of Prisoner of Azkaban: with no signature from the Dursleys, Harry gets to the village anyway by using the Marauder's Map and a secret passage that starts behind a one-eyed witch statue and comes out in the cellar of Honeydukes.

Shops and inns

  • Honeydukes, a sweetshop stocked with everything from Fizzing Whizzbees to Chocolate Frogs.
  • Zonko's Joke Shop, a favorite of the Weasley twins.
  • The Three Broomsticks, a warm, busy inn run by Madam Rosmerta and famous for its butterbeer.
  • The Hog's Head, a grimy pub kept by Aberforth Dumbledore, where the student group that became Dumbledore's Army first met.
  • Madam Puddifoot's, a frilly tea shop and the scene of Harry and Cho's uncomfortable Valentine's Day date.
  • Dervish and Banges, Gladrags Wizardwear and Scrivenshaft's Quill Shop round out the high street.

The Shrieking Shack

On the edge of the village stands the Shrieking Shack, said to be the most haunted building in Britain. In truth the screams came from Remus Lupin, who used the shack as a safe place to transform as a student, reaching it through a tunnel beneath the Whomping Willow. Years later it is also where Snape dies.

During the war

By the final book, Death Eaters have placed the village under a curfew enforced by a Caterwauling Charm. Aberforth hides Harry, Ron and Hermione and sends them into the castle through a passage behind his sister's portrait, setting up the Battle of Hogwarts.
